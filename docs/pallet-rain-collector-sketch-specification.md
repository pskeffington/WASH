# Pallet Rain Collector Sketch Specification

## Purpose

This document defines the visual and dimensional contract for all future sketches, diagrams, concept art, field drawings, and prototype illustrations of the pallet rainwater collector.

It exists to prevent drawings from drifting away from the current engineering concept.

## Reference architecture

All sketches must show an **elevated, freestanding pallet catchment** that gravity-feeds an existing barrel or cistern.

```text
ELEVATED PALLET / WOOD FRAME
        ↓
REPLACEABLE WATERPROOF LINER
        ↓
LOW-EDGE GUTTER
        ↓
SCREEN
        ↓
FIRST-FLUSH DIVERTER
        ↓
CONTINUOUS GRAVITY DROP
        ↓
BARREL / CISTERN INLET
        ↓
STORAGE
        ↓
CONTROLLED OVERFLOW
```

A pallet lying on the ground is **not** an acceptable depiction of the reference system.

## Mandatory elevation relationship

The drawing must visibly preserve this order:

```text
LOW EDGE OF CATCHMENT
        >
GUTTER OUTLET
        >
TRANSFER TEE / FIRST-FLUSH ENTRY
        >
BARREL OR CISTERN INLET
```

Every connection must step down in elevation.

The purpose is to avoid pumps, siphon dependence, lifting, and reverse-slope plumbing.

### Reference geometry for a 55-gallon drum

For a typical drum approximately 34-36 in high, sketches should begin with a catchment low-edge height of approximately:

```text
42-48 in above grade
```

and then adjust to the actual barrel, fittings, first-flush assembly, and gutter geometry.

This height is a starting point, not a universal requirement.

The preferred design rule is:

> Use the minimum stable elevation that still provides reliable gravity feed to the selected storage inlet.

## Pallet geometry

Reference pallet:

```text
48 x 40 in common pallet
surface area ≈ 13.3 ft2
```

Other pallet sizes are acceptable, but the drawing should not imply that all pallets are 48 x 48 in.

Reference operating angle:

```text
10-20 degrees from horizontal
nominal: ~15 degrees
```

The angle should be shallow enough to preserve horizontal rainfall interception while still draining reliably.

## Structural depiction

The sketch must show a credible support system.

Acceptable examples include:

- scrap-lumber legs;
- second pallet used as base or brace;
- diagonal wood bracing;
- masonry or concrete footing blocks;
- screened-rubble ballast outside the water path;
- stakes or anchors where site conditions permit.

The drawing should communicate:

- four-point or otherwise stable support;
- bracing against racking;
- broad footing;
- wind stability;
- no dependence on an aging house wall or roof.

Do not depict the pallet simply propped at an unstable angle.

## Water-contact surface

The pallet wood is structural material. The actual catchment surface should be shown as a **replaceable liner or membrane**.

Candidate prototype depictions may include:

- EPDM or pond liner;
- heavy polyethylene sheet;
- characterized roofing membrane;
- another known waterproof sheet appropriate to the intended research use.

The liner should visibly extend to the low edge and feed the gutter without pockets or reverse folds.

## No-paint rule

Future drawings and written sketch instructions must **not recommend painting, staining, sealing, spraying, or coating the pallet or nearby water-contact components as a routine design step**.

Do not include visual callouts such as:

```text
paint pallet
seal wood with paint
apply waterproof paint
coat frame
spray protective finish
```

The design instead relies on separation of functions:

```text
WOOD = STRUCTURE
LINER = WATER-CONTACT / WATERPROOF SURFACE
```

This avoids introducing unnecessary coatings or unknown compounds close to the collection path.

If future research evaluates a specific coating, it must be treated as a separately characterized material and not shown as the default architecture.

## Gutter depiction

Acceptable gutter concepts:

- split PVC pipe;
- conventional half-round gutter;
- bent sheet gutter where material provenance is suitable;
- supported folded-liner trough.

The gutter must appear below the catchment low edge and above the barrel inlet.

The drawing should show positive slope toward the outlet rather than a level trough.

## First-flush depiction

A first-flush assembly should be shown between the screen/gutter outlet and storage.

The preferred simple conceptual arrangement is:

```text
collector flow
      ↓
      T ─────→ later runoff to barrel
      ↓
first-flush standpipe
      ↓
slow drain / cleanout
```

The first-flush branch must not force water to climb back uphill into the storage line.

For a single pallet, initial prototype test volumes remain:

```text
0.25 gal
0.5 gal
1.0 gal
```

A drawing should not freeze one value as universal unless the sketch is explicitly for that tested configuration.

## Storage depiction

The storage vessel should be shown:

- below the collector outlet;
- on stable ground or a low broad base;
- with a closed or closable top;
- with screened/protected inlet where practical;
- with independent overflow;
- with no implication that the vessel itself must be elevated to receive water.

Storage may be represented as:

- 55-gallon drum;
- 200-L barrel;
- IBC tote;
- existing cistern;
- other characterized storage.

## Overflow depiction

Overflow should be shown leaving near the maximum storage waterline and routed toward a safe drainage area.

Acceptable visual endpoints include:

- rock apron;
- vegetated swale;
- rain garden;
- infiltration area;
- daylight discharge where safe and appropriate.

Do not show overflow concentrated onto an unstable slope, foundation, footpath, or latrine area.

## Required annotation block

Future technical sketches should include, where space permits:

```text
Reference pallet: actual dimensions recorded
Collector angle: 10-20 deg nominal
Low-edge height: field-adjusted to storage inlet
Gravity path: continuous downward fall
Water-contact surface: replaceable liner
Pallet/frame: structural only
Paint/coating: not recommended near collector
Storage: experimental/non-potable by default
```

## Performance annotation

For a nominal 48 x 40 in pallet at 15 degrees:

```text
surface area ≈ 13.33 ft2
horizontal projected area ≈ 12.88 ft2
1 in rain theoretical ≈ 8.0 gal
illustrative 80% recovery ≈ 6.4 gal
```

These are modeled values, not measured performance.

Sketches should label them as estimates unless field data are being shown.

## Minimum sketch views

A full engineering concept sheet should ideally show:

### 1. Perspective view

Communicate the complete system and elevated relationship.

### 2. Side elevation

Show:

- collector slope;
- low-edge height;
- gutter height;
- first-flush elevation;
- barrel inlet elevation;
- continuously descending water path.

### 3. Front view

Show:

- support width;
- bracing;
- gutter span;
- centered or side outlet.

### 4. Water-path schematic

Show the complete sequence from rainfall to overflow.

### 5. Pallet plan/top view

Show actual pallet dimensions and liner coverage.

## Sketch rejection conditions

A drawing should be revised before publication if it shows any of the following:

- pallet lying flat on the ground as the active collector;
- collector outlet below the storage inlet;
- pipe rising from gutter to barrel;
- first-flush assembly creating a reverse-slope path;
- barrel elevated unnecessarily to compensate for a low collector;
- unsupported or obviously unstable pallet frame;
- water flowing directly over aged pallet wood without a controlled liner;
- paint or coating recommended on or near the pallet as the waterproofing strategy;
- open unprotected storage where the architecture is presented as finished;
- no overflow route;
- potable-use implication without a separate validated treatment and water-quality pathway.

## Design intent

The sketch should make the underlying concept understandable at a glance:

```text
ABUNDANT WOOD PROVIDES STRUCTURE
        +
ELEVATION PROVIDES GRAVITY HEAD
        +
LINER PROVIDES CONTROLLED CATCHMENT
        +
SIMPLE PLUMBING PROVIDES TRANSFER
        +
EXISTING BARREL / CISTERN PROVIDES STORAGE
```

The result should look buildable from common materials without requiring roof repair, power, a pump, specialized framing, or painted/coated pallet surfaces.
