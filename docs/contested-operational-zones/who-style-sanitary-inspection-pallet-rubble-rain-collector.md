# WHO-Style Sanitary Inspection Form — Pallet/Rubble Rain Collector

## Purpose

This field form adapts WHO small-supply and rainwater sanitary-inspection principles to the WASH repo's pallet/rubble rain collector. It is designed for rapid use by humanitarian, public-health, engineering, utility, community, or other legitimate WASH responders.

This is a project field tool, not an official WHO form. It should be used alongside applicable local public-health requirements and the WHO rainwater sanitary-inspection package.

### First-flush rule

A plain vertical standpipe tee should **not** be assumed to isolate the first contaminated runoff automatically after filling.

Document the design as either:

```text
FF-M1 manual/simple diverter
or
FF-F1 floating-ball shutoff
```

and verify its actual function.

### Stop-use triggers

Investigate and downgrade use if:

- filtered turbidity rises sharply;
- outlet approaches inlet turbidity unexpectedly;
- filter flow increases suddenly;
- media is disturbed;
- seals or housings fail.

# I. Corrective-action matrix

| Finding | Immediate action |
|---|---|
| Suspected sewage/flood contamination | Isolate system; reject or downgrade water; clean/disinfect system as applicable; reassess source |
| Unknown chemical container | Remove from drinking-water pathway |
| Suspected asbestos/industrial rubble | Stop disturbance; isolate area; use screened alternative material |
| Torn/failed liner | Stop collection into drinking-water pathway; repair/replace; reinspect |
| Failed first flush | Divert water away from drinking-water storage until corrected |
| Filter bypass or sudden high flow | Stop higher-use claim; inspect filter/seals |
| Treated storage recontamination | Clean/disinfect storage; correct dispensing/closure failure; retest |
| Unexpected conductivity/salinity | Investigate source mixing, seawater/brackish input, or contamination; do not assume biological treatment solves it |
| Positive microbial indicator after final treatment | Stop drinking-water classification; investigate treatment and storage; apply validated corrective treatment |
| Unstable rubble pad or barrel stand | Remove personnel from failure zone; unload/secure if safe; rebuild support |

# K. Minimum reinspection triggers

Reinspect after:

- each major storm during commissioning;
- heavy dust/demolition event;
- nearby fire or industrial release;
- flooding/sewage intrusion;
- collector relocation;
- barrel replacement;
- liner repair;
- filter maintenance or media disturbance;
- abnormal flow/turbidity result;
- positive microbial result;
- visible settlement of rubble foundation;
- long shutdown before reuse.

## WHO/UNICEF alignment

This project form is structured around the same core concepts emphasized by WHO and UNICEF for small supplies and emergency household water treatment:

```text
SANITARY INSPECTION
+
SOURCE-TO-CONSUMER RISK CONTROL
+
SAFE STORAGE
+
OPERATIONAL MONITORING
+
CORRECTIVE ACTION
+
VERIFICATION
```

Primary guidance anchors include:

- WHO, *Sanitary inspection package (drinking-water): rainwater collection and storage* (2026).
- WHO, *Guidelines for drinking-water quality* (2026).
- WHO, *Guidelines for drinking-water quality: small water supplies* (2024).
- WHO, *Water safety plan: step-by-step risk management for drinking-water suppliers: training package* (2026).
- UNICEF emergency household water treatment and safe storage guidance.
