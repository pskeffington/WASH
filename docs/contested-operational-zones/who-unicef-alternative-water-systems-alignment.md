# WHO / UNICEF Alignment for Alternative Water Systems

## Purpose

This note aligns the WASH repo's alternative-water-system work with current WHO and UNICEF guidance for emergency, household, small-supply, and climate/conflict-resilient WASH delivery.

The central conclusion is that alternative systems should not be treated as isolated improvised devices. They should be managed as **small water supplies with catchment-to-consumer risk control**, safe storage, operational monitoring, corrective actions, and explicit use classification.

---

## 1. Core WHO alignment

WHO's 2026 *Guidelines for drinking-water quality* reinforce a catchment-to-consumer framework built around:

- health-based targets;
- risk-based management;
- water safety planning;
- operational monitoring;
- independent surveillance.

For this repo, that means every alternative system should be documented as:

```text
SOURCE
  ↓
COLLECTION
  ↓
PRETREATMENT
  ↓
TREATMENT
  ↓
DISINFECTION AS REQUIRED
  ↓
SAFE STORAGE
  ↓
DISTRIBUTION
  ↓
MONITOR / CORRECT / RECLASSIFY
```

Measured performance, not appearance or assumptions, should determine use classification.

---

## 2. Small-water-supply framework

WHO's guidance for small water supplies is directly applicable to decentralized household and community systems.

The relevant principles are:

- context-appropriate health protection;
- proactive hazard identification;
- sanitary inspection;
- water safety planning;
- surveillance;
- simple management arrangements that fit limited-resource settings.

This provides a strong framework for:

- pallet/tarp rain collectors;
- barrel banks;
- household filter trains;
- community modular nodes;
- gravity-driven treatment;
- passive atmospheric-water prototypes.

These should be treated as **managed small supplies**, even when the infrastructure is improvised.

## 3. WHO rainwater sanitary-inspection alignment

WHO released a dedicated sanitary-inspection package for rainwater collection and storage in July 2026.

The package contains:

- a sanitary inspection form;
- a technical fact sheet;
- management advice for operations, maintenance, monitoring, and corrective action.

This is highly relevant to the WASH repo.

The repo should mirror that structure for every rainwater prototype:

### RW-SI-1 — sanitary inspection

Inspect:

- catchment cleanliness;
- proximity to contamination sources;
- water-contact material condition;
- gutter integrity;
- first-flush function;
- storage closure;
- overflow condition;
- drainage around storage;
- animal/insect access;
- raw/treated separation.

### RW-SI-2 — technical record

Record:

- collector area;
- slope;
- material;
- first-flush volume;
- storage capacity;
- measured yield;
- treatment train;
- maintenance interval.

### RW-SI-3 — corrective action

Examples:

- clean catchment;
- increase first flush after heavy dust;
- repair liner;
- replace contaminated vessel;
- repair screened vent;
- redirect overflow;
- downgrade water use if treatment/quality is uncertain.

## 4. Water Safety Plan adaptation

WHO's 2026 Water Safety Plan training package reinforces proactive, stepwise risk management from catchment to consumer.

The WASH repo can adapt this to a compact Alternative Water Safety Plan (A-WSP).

### A-WSP 1 — describe system

Document:

- source;
- collection;
- storage;
- treatment;
- distribution;
- users.

### A-WSP 2 — identify hazards

Examples:

- sewage intrusion;
- dust/debris;
- unsuitable catchment coating;
- metals from roofing;
- unknown barrel history;
- microbial contamination;
- salinity/nitrate;
- floodwater;
- filter bypass;
- storage recontamination.

### A-WSP 3 — define controls

Examples:

- source rejection;
- controlled liner;
- first flush;
- settling;
- filtration;
- disinfection;
- covered storage;
- source isolation;
- field testing.

### A-WSP 4 — operational monitoring

Track:

- flow;
- turbidity;
- pH;
- conductivity/TDS;
- chlorine residual where applicable;
- microbial field indicators;
- liner/barrel/filter condition.

### A-WSP 5 — corrective action

Every abnormal reading or damaged component should trigger a predefined response.

### A-WSP 6 — verification

Use stronger laboratory or independent testing when making higher-confidence drinking-water claims.

## 5. UNICEF emergency HWTS criteria

UNICEF's household water treatment and safe storage work defines useful emergency-system product characteristics.

The WASH repo should explicitly score designs against:

### affordability

Can a household or responder build/deploy the unit at low cost?

### intuitive use

Can operation be explained simply?

### fail-safe behavior

Can failure be detected before users assume water is safe?

### durability

Can the unit withstand emergency/displacement conditions?

### low logistics footprint

How much imported mass/volume is required?

### safe storage

Does the design prevent recontamination after treatment?

### service life

How long can it operate before major replacement?

### low consumable burden

Does the system require continuous chemicals, cartridges, fuel, or proprietary parts?

### microbial protection

If claiming drinking-water performance, does the health-critical stage have evidence against bacteria, viruses, and protozoa appropriate to intended use?

## 6. UNICEF gravity-driven treatment alignment

UNICEF has specifically investigated gravity-driven membrane systems because they can lower maintenance and eliminate pressurized pumping/backflush requirements in some configurations.

This aligns with the repo's architecture:

```text
IMPROVISED / LOCAL COLLECTION
        ↓
PASSIVE PRETREATMENT
        ↓
STANDARDIZED GRAVITY HEALTH-CRITICAL BARRIER
        ↓
SAFE STORAGE
```

This hybrid approach is stronger than attempting to improvise every treatment stage.

The upstream components can use:

- rubble;
- pallets;
- local timber;
- tarps;
- barrels;
- simple gravel/sand pretreatment.

The final health-critical barrier should become increasingly controlled and evidence-backed.

---

## 7. WHO household-treatment alignment

WHO recognizes household water treatment and safe storage as an important emergency response intervention when improved or piped supplies are unsafe or unreliable.

This reinforces three repo principles:

1. household-scale treatment is legitimate as an interim/emergency layer;
2. safe storage is inseparable from treatment;
3. correct and consistent use matters as much as laboratory technology performance.

Therefore prototype evaluation must include not only treatment efficiency but also:

- ease of use;
- cleaning burden;
- error modes;
- safe dispensing;
- labeling;
- operator adherence.

---

## 8. WHO HWT technology-performance alignment

WHO's International Scheme to Evaluate Household Water Treatment Technologies provides performance categories for products evaluated against microbial reduction criteria.

The WASH repo should not claim WHO-equivalent protection for improvised filters unless the specific product/system has appropriate evidence.

Use three evidence lanes:

### E0 — improvised/non-confirmatory

Claims limited to:

- debris removal;
- sediment reduction;
- turbidity change;
- hydraulic performance.

### E1 — evidence-supported component

A standardized ceramic, membrane, or treatment product with manufacturer/independent performance data.

### E2 — WHO-evaluated / independently validated component

Use documented performance criteria from WHO or equivalent independent evaluation.

The system-level claim still depends on correct installation, use, storage, and source-water conditions.

## 9. Alternative-system service ladder

The repo should use a staged service ladder rather than jumping immediately to potable claims.

### AS-0 — source preservation

Protect intact safe supplies.

### AS-1 — bounded non-potable water

Collection/storage for uses such as cleaning or other context-appropriate non-potable functions.

### AS-2 — treatment-required water

Source collected and protected but not yet suitable for drinking.

### AS-3 — controlled household-treatment system

Known treatment chain with operational monitoring.

### AS-4 — validated intended-use supply

Water meets the applicable evidence/testing requirement for the declared use.

This should remain linked to existing repo classifications:

```text
REJECT
NON-POTABLE / BOUNDED USE
TREATMENT REQUIRED
TREATMENT RESEARCH ONLY
VALIDATED FOR INTENDED USE
```

## 10. Minimum design package for an alternative water system

Every prototype should eventually include:

### system description

- source type;
- collector type;
- storage volume;
- treatment train;
- intended population;
- intended use.

### sanitary inspection

- site risks;
- catchment risks;
- storage risks;
- drainage risks;
- contamination pathways.

### operational monitoring

- yield;
- flow;
- turbidity;
- pH;
- conductivity;
- residual disinfectant where relevant;
- microbial indicators where relevant.

### maintenance plan

- cleaning schedule;
- first-flush reset;
- filter service;
- storage cleaning;
- replacement parts.

### corrective-action plan

- what triggers shutdown;
- what triggers non-potable downgrade;
- what requires replacement;
- what requires laboratory escalation.

### use classification

Every outlet must be labeled by intended use.

## 11. Passive-system alignment

WHO/UNICEF guidance does not require powered systems by default. In emergencies, simplicity, durability, safe storage, and low logistical burden are major advantages.

This supports prioritizing:

- rainwater catchment;
- gravity conveyance;
- settling;
- gravity roughing;
- biosand/slow sand where appropriate;
- ceramic filtration;
- gravity membrane systems;
- solar disinfection where appropriate;
- gravity distribution.

Powered components should be reserved for functions that physics or water chemistry actually require, such as:

- deep pumping;
- desalination;
- certain membrane processes;
- UV systems;
- high-throughput community distribution.

---

## 12. Transition from emergency to stabilized service

WHO emergency guidance and UNICEF resilience work support progression rather than permanent dependence on improvised systems.

The WASH repo should define:

```text
SURVIVAL
  ↓
STABILIZATION
  ↓
CONTROLLED SMALL SUPPLY
  ↓
REHABILITATED / RESILIENT SERVICE
```

Alternative systems should remain useful as redundancy even after centralized service returns.

Examples:

- rainwater remains a supplemental source;
- barrel banks remain emergency storage;
- passive filters remain household fallback;
- solar/gravity components reduce future operating burden.

## 13. Updated WHO/UNICEF-aligned gates

### WU-0 — context

Define population, source, intended use, hazards, and existing infrastructure.

### WU-1 — sanitary inspection

Complete catchment/source/storage risk screen.

### WU-2 — system design

Define source-to-consumer architecture and material controls.

### WU-3 — operational monitoring

Define measurable parameters and limits.

### WU-4 — treatment evidence

Classify each treatment component by evidence level.

### WU-5 — safe storage

Verify closed storage, controlled outlet, cleaning, and contamination isolation.

### WU-6 — corrective action

Define explicit responses to failed monitoring or damaged components.

### WU-7 — verification

Conduct field/lab confirmation appropriate to intended use.

### WU-8 — service classification

Assign final use status and communicate it clearly.

### WU-9 — resilience transition

Document how the system integrates with restored municipal or community service.

## Working conclusion

WHO and UNICEF guidance strongly supports the repo's direction when alternative systems are treated as managed, monitored small water supplies rather than as improvised collection devices.

The aligned architecture is:

```text
LOW-RESOURCE / PASSIVE COLLECTION
        ↓
SANITARY INSPECTION
        ↓
CONTROLLED WATER-CONTACT PATH
        ↓
EVIDENCE-BACKED TREATMENT
        ↓
SAFE STORAGE
        ↓
OPERATIONAL MONITORING
        ↓
CORRECTIVE ACTION
        ↓
VERIFIED USE CLASSIFICATION
```

This provides a defensible pathway for delivering alternative water systems during emergencies while preserving public-health controls and supporting eventual transition back to resilient community or municipal service.
